# Framing: fixed stage (built and playtested)

A front view of one set, like a theatre or a TV studio, with a 2D camera that pans and zooms
between moments.

- **Layers,** back to front: backdrop, far furniture, the place the subject stands (with its
  railing or counter in front of them), side positions for actors who come and go, and a near
  counter or table across the bottom quarter, kept nearly black for the player's hand or options.
  Each layer is its own image so the camera can move between them.
- **Camera:** home is the full set. Moments zoom to about ×1.4 on the subject, with eased moves
  (0.3–0.5 s). Every move has a way home, triggered by the moment ending, never only by the player.
- **Actors off until they act.** Secondary characters enter from off screen when they act and leave
  after; a crowd or audience is seen only in the moment it matters.
- **Subject placement:** the speaking character sits left of centre, about 60% of screen height,
  with their line in a panel beside them, pointing at them.
- **The player's options** sit on the near counter: a hand of cards or a row of choices, lifted on
  focus.

**Pitfalls in this framing:** furniture in front of a character hides the reaction poses (keep
railings below the chest); a zoomed shot crops the interface (lay the interface out in screen space,
not on the set).
