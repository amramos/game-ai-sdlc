# Framing: fixed stage, from the audience (built in a prototype)

The player sits in the stalls and looks into the box: the default framing for this look, the one it
was built in, and the one demo.html renders.

- **Camera:** perspective, vertical field of view 34°, 11.5 units in front of the frame, eye 1.3
  above the opening's centre, looking at a point 0.5 below that centre in the middle of the stage.
  The frame fills about 87% of the screen's width; a strip of `house` shows around it. On screens
  narrower than 16:9, widen the field of view so the frame keeps its width; never crop the pillars.
- **Movement:** none but pointer parallax (±0.25 unit sideways, ±0.12 vertically) and a short shake
  on impacts (±0.05 for 0.2 s). Held moments may push in to a field of view of 26–28° on one
  figure, with the move eased over 0.4 s and a way home when the moment ends.
- **What the core changes:** the frame is the interface's furniture. Pillars (about 170 px wide at
  720p) carry pinned slips: inventory and resources on one side, the scene list or objectives on
  the other. Dialogue and choices fly in on wires into the upper third of the opening, above the
  cast's heads; hanging signs clear the valance by at least their own border, as seen from the
  camera. Name tags sit 8–10 px under each figure's feet; a detail card goes beside the
  figure under the pointer, inside the opening.
- **Composition:** figures stand mid-stage so their heads sit at about the middle of the screen and
  their feet in the lower third, leaving the upper third of the opening for flown cards. Keep the
  floor in front of the cast free: it is where entrances, props and dropped slips land.
- **Light:** the key from front-left, high, so shadows fall back and right onto the rows; the
  footlights throw the cast's shadows upward onto the back cloth, strongest in the dark schemes.
- **Scene changes:** fly the back cloth and rows out and in, slide the cast to the wings; the frame
  and the interface on it stay put.

**Pitfalls seen in this framing:** choice cards in a strip along the bottom edge read as menus,
not props; flown cards hung in the same lane overlapped, the back text showing through; small
figures on a detailed set overlapped and were hard to target; a stage dimmed for tension took
lit-only text with it. During a held moment, dimming the house and fading the side props to a few
percent made the moment unmissable.
