# Framing: side view, a scrolling panorama (not yet built)

The stage becomes a wide panorama that scrolls past as the cast travels: a journey, a chase or a
platformer side view, still inside the frame.

- **Camera:** as in fixed-stage, but the set moves, not the camera. The frame stays fixed; the
  rows and back cloth scroll sideways, each at its own speed: back cloth 0.15, far row 0.35, mid
  row 0.6, near row 0.85, the cast's floor 1.0 of the travel speed.
- **What the core changes:** rows become long strips (at least 3× the opening's width) or loops
  painted to tile, with the seam hidden behind a wing or a tall element. Rows can also be printed
  on slowly turning rollers (a cylinder radius 0.45 at each row's depth) for clouds,
  crowds or passing fields.
- **Cast:** the travelling figure stays at 35–40% of the opening's width, facing the direction of
  travel; others enter on slides from the wing ahead and leave by the wing behind.
- **Light:** fixed to the stage, not to the scrolling set, so shadows stay put while the prints
  move under them; a light change marks the passing of time or a new place.
- **Interface:** as in fixed-stage; a distance or progress slip pinned to the header.

**Expected pitfalls:** tiled strips show seams (break them with a taller element every loop);
scroll speeds that are too close make the rows read as one sheet (keep at least 0.2 between
neighbours); a scrolling floor under a still figure looks like sliding unless the figure has a
walk rock (±2° at the step rate).
