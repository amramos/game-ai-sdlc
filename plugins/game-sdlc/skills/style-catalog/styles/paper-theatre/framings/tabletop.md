# Framing: tabletop, the toy theatre on a table (not yet built)

The camera pulls back to show the whole theatre standing on a table in a room, so a narrator's or
the player's hand can reach in, move pieces or turn the scene.

- **Camera:** perspective, field of view 40–45°, 25–30° above the table, 15–25° to one side of the
  theatre's axis, so the box's side and depth show. The frame fills 55–65% of the screen's width.
  Return to a straight front view (the fixed-stage camera) for scenes that need the full picture.
- **What the core changes:** the box gains a visible outside: side walls and a top in printed card
  with the same border and edge, and the wings' backs show (printed plain or with stage notes). The
  `house` role becomes the room: keep it at least 2:1 against the frame. The table is a large
  surface in a tint role with grain hatching.
- **Light:** a room light (a lamp or a window) as the key from above and to one side, plus the
  theatre's own footlights. The stage stays brighter than the room so the eye goes into the box.
- **Hand and props:** a hand cut-out (or a real 3D hand in the mixed-cast variation) enters from a
  screen edge at 1.2–1.5× the cast's scale; props the player can take lie on the table beside the
  box, as cut-outs on card feet.
- **Interface:** paper slips lying on the table or propped against the box, not pinned to the frame.

**Expected pitfalls:** seen at an angle, the cut-outs' flatness shows (turn them toward the camera
by half the camera's angle); a busy room steals the eye from the stage (keep it a value step darker
and under the prints' chroma); text on slips lying flat foreshortens (prop them up at 60° or more).
